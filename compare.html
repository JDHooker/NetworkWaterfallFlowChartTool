Matching weights

Higher size weight trusts packet sizes more; higher position weight keeps matches near the same point in each capture; a higher gap penalty prefers pairing over leaving packets unmatched.

How matching works

Each side is turned into a list of packets (or messages) after the filters above. Every one gets a direction relative to the client host, a kind (SYN, DNS query, TLS Client Hello, TLS data …), its payload size and any names it mentions (SNI, DNS, Host). The two lists are then aligned in order, like a text diff: pairs score for same direction, same kind, similar size, shared names and similar position; leaving a packet unmatched costs the gap penalty. Packets in different directions are never paired.

Click a row, then Link or Unlink to correct the result; links stay fixed while the rest is re-aligned around them.

Compare two captures

Open two .pcap or .pcapng files, for example the same curl https://www.youtube.com once through a proxy or tunnel and once directly. Their packets are lined up side by side so you can see which ones correspond, what only one side has, and how much the tunnel adds. Files are read in your browser and never uploaded.
